As a user, I run `tcw taxonomy show <path>` to read an entry: its full identifier, the project that owns it, its name, kind, feature vocabulary refs, relatesTo links, attachments, and its description. The text form starts with `full-id: <project>.taxonomy/<id-path>` and `owner: <project>`. I name the entry by its full identifier, its `tcw://` address, or its id-path alone, which names the one entry at that path in my project's view: combined field by field from my project's own entry there, if any, and the ones it inherits, and named by the full identifier of the nearest project that has one. A full identifier names that project's own entry as that project sees it, so `shared.taxonomy/invoice` is `shared`'s entry, never my override of it, and an entry of any other project TCW can locate can be read the same way. When the entry overrides one, the text form prints `overrides: <full id>` after `owner:` and `parent:`, and the record carries `overrides`, a full identifier or `null`. `--json` prints `{"schema": 2, "term": {...}}` with the same fields, under `full-id` and `owner`. A path that matches no entry exits 4, and a project ID TCW cannot find exits 1. An id-path is never ambiguous: where two parents that do not inherit from each other both have an entry at it, the one later in my project's load order is used, with a warning on standard error.

A term's record carries its `parent`, and its attachments are the files in its `resources/` folder.

When a parent is not on this machine, `show` of one of my project's own entries still works: it prints the entry's own declaration alone, with `partial: inherited content could not be read` in place of an `overrides:` line, and the JSON record carries `"partial": true` and no `overrides`. A warning on standard error says only my project's own declarations are shown, and it exits 0. `show` of an entry my project inherits stops with exit 7.
